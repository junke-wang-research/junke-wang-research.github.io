Research

Research thrusts

ESIL combines thermal-fluid science, controls, cyber-physical energy systems, sensing, and data-driven methods to improve the performance and grid responsiveness of building and thermal energy systems.

01

HVAC & Thermal Energy Systems

Dynamic behavior, design, and operation of heat pumps, vapor-compression systems, water-heating systems, thermal storage, hydronic systems, and integrated thermal-energy technologies.

02

Advanced Control & Optimization

Model predictive control, supervisory control, control-oriented system identification, optimization under operational constraints, and intelligent/autonomous operation.

03

Grid-Interactive Buildings & Demand Flexibility

Flexible building loads, load shifting, demand response, building-grid coordination, and control of HVAC and other distributed energy resources to support grid needs.

04

Fault Detection, Diagnostics & Automated Correction

Detection and isolation of performance degradation, data-driven and model-based diagnostics, virtual sensing, and control actions that restore efficient operation.

05

Physics-Based, Data-Driven & AI-Enabled Modeling

Reduced-order thermal models, digital twins, machine learning, hybrid physics/data models, state and parameter estimation, and predictive analytics for control and decision-making.

06

Experimental Validation & Field Demonstration

Controlled-environment HVAC testing, sensor and data-acquisition development, laboratory-scale controller validation, and performance evaluation in real buildings and energy systems.

Current directions

Near-term ESIL projects are expected to emphasize heat-pump controls and performance, demand flexibility, advanced diagnostics, and experimentally validated modeling and control methods. The lab is intentionally structured to connect algorithm development with measurable system performance.

Heat pumps & electrification

Control and optimization of heat-pump-based systems under variable loads, weather, equipment constraints, and multi-service operation.

Controls that transfer to hardware

Develop control methods that can be tested with real sensors, actuators, controllers, and thermal equipment rather than remaining simulation-only.

Flexible & resilient operation

Use building thermal dynamics and controllable loads to provide flexibility while maintaining comfort, efficiency, reliability, and equipment-aware operation.
